Revenue Cycle Management Platform

Keplr Vision · Built from June 2025, first demonstration January 2026, UAT from March 2026

Summary

I architected and led development of a full-stack internal Revenue Cycle Management workflow platform that centralized insurance-payment posting, issue resolution, access control, auditability, and operational reporting. The UAT-stage platform processed production data from seven bank files (five daily and two weekly) covering all 11 practice-management systems, classified approximately 3,000 actionable insurance-payment records from approximately 12,000 daily banking transactions, and replaced fragmented Excel-based workflows with governed queues and record-level history.

Context and problem

Revenue Cycle Management staff identified insurance-carrier payments from multiple bank files and made sure each was posted in the right practice-management system so claims could be closed and revenue realized. The work was fragmented across Excel files, systems, and teams. Records could be deleted, hidden by filters, stranded in old workbook versions, or overlooked during issue resolution, and leadership had no centralized view of workload, productivity, carrier classification, or unresolved payments.

Architecture

The analytical backend needed several workarounds to behave transactionally. The proof of concept worked for UAT but was not the intended long-term architecture; about a week before I left, I began a hybrid redesign on Databricks AppKit and Lakebase that would keep Databricks for file processing and classification and synchronize candidate rows into Lakebase for faster transactional behavior.

What I built

Event-driven bank-file ingestion

A separate Flask Databricks App, Upload Manager, showed seven bank-specific upload tiles, accepted CSV and some XLSX files, converted them to Parquet, uploaded them to a Databricks Volume, and triggered the matching ingestion pipeline only after a successful conversion and upload. It supported drag-and-drop of one file per tile or batch upload of up to 10 files, refreshed pipeline status every 15 seconds until completion or failure, listed the 10 most recent uploads with full history available, blocked uploads when a matching file already existed, and checked that the transaction date range agreed with the file-naming convention.

Insurance-payment classification

Seven bank-specific formats were conformed into one process. All classification mappings lived in a single table and supported one to six conditions, including exclusion conditions, over description text, account mappings, transaction types, and other bank-specific fields. That reduced roughly 12,000 deposits, withdrawals, and transfers a day to roughly 3,000 candidate insurance-payment workflows.

Workflow, access, and concurrency

Each user saw a cash-posting queue filtered through mapping tables to their authorized practices, practice groups, and practice-management systems. A user locked a record before opening its editing workflow; the first user got the lock and a second user saw an explanatory message. Generated unique transaction IDs prevented duplicates and tracked records across the application, along with workflow state, assignment, posting status, completion, and issue-resolution history.

Escalation, auditability, and reporting

Users could flag problems on individual payments, with threaded notes and communication history stored on the record. Escalated records surfaced to supervisors and stayed in a dedicated flow until resolved, while incomplete items kept appearing in the primary workflow. Nothing disappeared through workbook deletion, filters, or file rotation. The platform reported dollar amounts, payment counts, posting volume, user productivity, workflow status, and outstanding issues, and gave Treasury more granular visibility into carrier payments, patient payments, and other parts of manual deposits.

Status and boundaries

UAT-stage, placed on hold before production adoption

  • UAT ran on production data in the production environment, but the business workflow was never rolled out as a production operation. The application was placed on hold near my layoff.
  • All 11 practice-management systems were represented in the data; approximately three were actively tested in the first UAT round.
  • The Lakebase redesign reached only a small visual portion, and the proposed two-stage classification workflow (mapping rules first, then an LLM suggesting classifications for the remainder into a review table for an authorized non-SQL user to promote) was designed but not completed.
