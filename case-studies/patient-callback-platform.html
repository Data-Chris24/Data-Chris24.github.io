Patient Callback Platform

Keplr Vision · Pilot September 2025, broad rollout March 2026, supported through July 2026

Summary

I architected and delivered a production patient-callback workflow platform that replaced error-prone Excel workbooks with governed patient assignment, concurrency controls, automated nightly data preparation, Dayforce-driven access provisioning, and near-real-time operational reporting. Across its lifetime from the September 2025 pilot through July 2026, the Flask/Delta and later AppKit/Lakebase versions supported 54 active practices, 69 enabled practices, more than 250 active users, more than 20,000 patient interactions, and 1,930 booked appointments.

Context and problem

Marketing maintained a callback process assembled from analytics code it did not fully own and from manual third-party exports of patient, schedule, and marketing-preference data. CSV ingestion and incomplete patient-to-sales relationships produced duplicate, missing, and inaccurate records.

The process selected patients who had visited the practice within the prior three years, had no exam in the prior 12 months, and had no future exam scheduled. Marketing scored scheduling likelihood from A through E, exported the results to Excel, applied conditional formatting and data validation, added fields for three callback attempts, and distributed a protected workbook to each practice.

Practice employees then searched for high-scoring patients and recorded attempts in shared rows. The workbook could not reliably prevent two people calling the same patient, free-form notes and caller initials made attribution unreliable, and leadership metrics required slow consolidation across workbooks.

Architecture

The original production version ran as a Python Flask application deployed on Databricks Apps against Delta tables and a Databricks SQL Warehouse, with candidate-selection and workflow logic in stored procedures. The modernized version runs on Databricks AppKit with Lakebase/Postgres, synchronized from the same Delta candidate pipeline.

What I built

Candidate selection and concurrency

In the Flask and Delta version, a stored procedure filtered to the user's selected practice, excluded completed, deleted, and waiting records (a three-business-day wait after the prior attempt), sorted by fewest attempts and then highest score, randomly chose from the top 10% to reduce competing locks, and updated the lock flag only when it was still unset. The record was returned only when exactly one row changed; otherwise the app retried. In the Lakebase version, PostgreSQL transactional locking selects and locks the next eligible row in one step, so simultaneous edits cannot happen.

Automated candidate-data pipeline

I combined patient, schedule, marketing-preference, sales, and score data, refactored the inherited sales-data logic, and corrected patient-to-sales relationships. The automated dataset removed the dependency on manually refreshed Marketing files that had introduced duplicates during the pilot. It first ran on request and was running nightly by the time I left. Its readiness in late January 2026 enabled the broader March 2026 rollout.

Dayforce-driven provisioning and RBAC

As a Databricks account and workspace administrator, I identified active employees at enrolled practices, selected eligible roles, categorized them as standard or administrative users, used the Databricks AccountClient capability to add missing accounts, and maintained both the application-user table and the user-to-practice mapping table. Employees assigned to several practices got one record. Terminated employees were soft-removed by default, with a configurable hard removal. The logic was split into a reusable AccountClient notebook and a callback-specific permissions notebook so other applications could adopt it.

Monitoring, recovery, and cutover

Pipeline-failure email alerts, Databricks execution monitoring, application logs in a Databricks Volume, Delta recovery for the original candidate table, Lakebase point-in-time recovery, and usage and outcome reporting. The hard cutover from Delta to Lakebase took about 10 hours and shipped with a leadership-controlled path back to the Delta-backed version.

Not every enabled practice completed training or began using the application, and practices whose practice-management systems lacked patient-level data in Databricks could not be enabled.

The migration to Postgres on Lakebase was the most important step in winning over end users: it made the app faster and enabled more functionality without affecting existing reporting pipelines or reports.

Status and boundaries

Production, supported through July 2026

  • Standardized callback workflows, prevented concurrent edits and duplicate calls, and gave practice and company leadership near-real-time visibility into work performed and outcomes.
  • I have not measured a quantified reduction in spreadsheet effort, errors, or duplicate calls against the old workbooks, so I do not claim one.
  • The original A-to-E patient-scoring algorithm came from Marketing; I automated the dataset that carried it.
  • Cost monitoring and some modernization features blocked by HIPAA-compliant workspace restrictions were planned but not completed.
